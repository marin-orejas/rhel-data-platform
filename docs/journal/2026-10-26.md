# 2026-10-26: Point-in-time recovery by hand, then pgBackRest

I wanted to bring db1 back to a set time after a bad change. First I did it by hand, with WAL archived to db2 and a base backup, and the outage took 9 min 31 s, almost all of it my typing. In the evening I moved everything to pgBackRest, and the same kind of restore took 1.1 s.

## What I did
- Turned on WAL archiving on db1 (started yesterday): every full 16 MiB segment goes to `/mnt/db2/backup/pgsql/wal`.
- SELinux blocked the archiver on `nfs_t`, so I built the local module `pg_archive_nfs` with `audit2allow`.
- Took a base backup with `pg_basebackup`: 4.3 MB compressed, 1.3 s, checked with `pg_verifybackup -n`.
- Shut down db2 while db1 wrote three segments. The archiver waited, and all three arrived 10 s after db2 was back.
- Dropped a test table and restored db1 in place to the time before it, paused, checked the data, then promoted on timeline 2.
- Added `checks/13-pitr.sh`. It passed after a reboot of both hosts.
- In the evening I installed pgBackRest 2.60 from the PGDG "common" repository, with its repository on db2.
- Created the stanza `main` and switched `archive_command` to `pgbackrest archive-push`. `pg_archive_nfs` went to version 1.1.
- Took a full, a diff and an incr backup: 17.8 s, 1.4 s and 1.5 s.
- Added `pgbackrest@.service` and two timers: a full on Sunday, a diff on the other nights, both at 01:00. Two fulls are kept.
- Dropped a table with 300,000 rows and restored to 5 s before the drop with one `pgbackrest restore`. db1 is now on timeline 3.
- Added `checks/14-pgbackrest.sh`. Checks 13 and 14 pass after a reboot of both hosts.

## What broke
- **With db2 down, `failed_count` did not change.** A `hard` NFS mount makes the archiver wait instead of fail, so monitoring has to watch the age of `last_archived_time` and the `.ready` files in `pg_wal`. A long outage would fill `/pgdata`.
- **The restore lost a good row.** The target cuts off everything after it, and I had added that row 4 s after the target. In a real incident I would find the exact time of the bad commit with `pg_waldump`.
- **The new SELinux rules worked in permissive mode, but archiving failed in enforcing.** `getattr` was missing: on the empty repository the directory did not exist yet, so nothing was logged. `audit2allow` only sees what actually ran.
- **After the third full, the oldest restore point was gone.** Retention expires the WAL of the removed backups too.
