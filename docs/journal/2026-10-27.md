# 2026-10-27: Streaming replication to a standby on db2

I wanted db2 to follow db1 change by change, as a read-only copy. It now streams WAL through a slot on db1, and the first copy took 0.8 s. One result I did not expect: right after a restart, db2 answered a query with the data from before it was stopped.

Phase 3 · Docs: [PostgreSQL](../architecture/postgresql.md), [ADR 0007](../decisions/0007-asynchronous-replication-with-a-capped-slot.md) · Runbook: [Build a standby with pg_basebackup](../runbooks/build-a-standby.md) · Checks: [15](../../checks/15-replication.sh)

## What I did
- Gave db2 its own 5 GiB LV on `/pgdata`, with the same layout, SELinux rules, port and drop-in as db1.
- Created the role `replicator` (`REPLICATION`, no superuser) and a `replication` line in `pg_hba.conf` for db2 only.
- Created the slot `db2` on db1 and capped it with `max_slot_wal_keep_size = 1GB`.
- Copied db1 with `pg_basebackup -R -S db2`: 52,552 kB in 0.8 s. `primary_conninfo` got `passfile=`, no password.
- Started db2. It streams on timeline 3, a row from db1 shows up on db2, and `CREATE TABLE` fails as read-only.
- Inserted a million rows in one transaction (2.8 s) and sampled lag every 0.5 s: at most 17.9 MB and 0.33 s behind.
- Stopped db2 and inserted 300,000 more rows. The slot kept 47 MB of WAL, and db2 caught up after the start.
- Added `checks/15-replication.sh`. Checks 06, 07 and 15 pass after a reboot of both hosts.

## What broke
- **My `psql` test asked for the `replicator` password, but `pg_basebackup` did not.** For physical replication the tools connect with `dbname=replication`, and that is what `.pgpass` matches. My test had no `dbname`, so libpq looked for a line with the user name.
- **db2 returned 1,000,000 rows when db1 had 1,300,000.** A hot standby takes queries as soon as it is consistent, before it has caught up. Reads should go to a standby only after its lag is checked.
- **On db2, `now() - pg_last_xact_replay_timestamp()` said 26 min while it was 0 bytes behind.** It is the time since the last commit on db1, not lag. I read lag in bytes on db1.
- **Check 15 failed after a restart of db2: `the walreceiver is streaming`.** The walreceiver puts `streaming` in its process title only after it writes new WAL, so with db1 idle the title is just `postgres: walreceiver`. The check now looks for the process, and db1 checks the streaming state.
