# 2026-11-05: TLS with a lab CA

I wanted every login between db1 and db2 to check who is on the other side. The certificates now come from my own lab CA, the role `postgresql` puts them in place with the keys from Vault, and check 22 passes 26 of 26 after a reboot of both hosts. Before, the standby had come back after a reboot with `sslmode=prefer`: TLSv1.3, but it would have taken any server at the address of db1.

Phase 4 · Docs: [PostgreSQL TLS](../architecture/postgresql/tls.md), [Role postgresql](../architecture/ansible/postgresql.md) · Runbook: [Fail over to db2 and bring db1 back](../runbooks/fail-over-to-db2.md) · Checks: [22](../../checks/22-tls.sh)

## What I did
- Made a CA on the desktop, its key with a passphrase, and signed EC P-256 certificates for db1 and db2 with their names and IPs.
- On db1 by hand first. `openssl s_client -starttls postgres` gave TLSv1.3, `CN=db1` and `Verification: OK`.
- With the CA in `~/.postgresql/root.crt` on db2, `report` got in with `verify-full`. The system CA bundle gave `certificate verify failed`.
- Gave the walreceiver on db2 `sslmode=verify-full` with `ALTER SYSTEM`, and it streamed again after the reload.
- Then as code: certificates in `group_vars` and `host_vars`, keys from `ansible-vault encrypt_string`, a new `tls.yml`.
- The key task has `no_log` and `diff: false`. A guard checks the served certificate with `s_client`, only in a real run.
- Eight `pg_hba.conf` rules are `hostssl` now, and `sslmode=disable` from db2 gets `… no encryption`. Second run: `changed=0`.
- db1 got `verify-full` too, for after a failover. A fake db2 (`hostaddr` of db1) was refused before the password. Added check 22.

## What broke
- **`Password for user replicator:` in a replication test from db1 to db2, with the line in `.pgpass`.** The test had no `dbname=replication`, so libpq looked for the database `replicator` in `.pgpass`. The walreceiver adds `dbname=replication` by itself, so only a test by hand needs it.
- **`prefer` to the fake db2 printed two `FATAL` lines, first `SSL encryption`, then `no encryption`.** After a refused TLS login, libpq tries again without TLS. A fake server just accepts the second try, so `prefer` protects against nothing.
