# 2026-10-29: Failover to db2 and back with pg_rewind

I wanted db2 to take over when db1 fails, and db1 to come back without a new base backup. I killed PostgreSQL on db1 with `kill -9` while a client wrote a row every 0.2 s: writes stopped for 5 min 51 s, almost all of it the steps before the promote, and no row was lost. The first reboot after it all failed check 06 on db2, because of one `disable --now` I typed during the switch back.

Phase 3 · Docs: [PostgreSQL](../architecture/postgresql.md) · Runbook: [Fail over to db2 and bring db1 back](../runbooks/fail-over-to-db2.md) · Checks: [16](../../checks/16-failover.sh)

## What I did
- Prepared the way back: a role `rewind` with only the four functions `pg_rewind` needs, and `pg_hba.conf` and `.pgpass` on both hosts.
- Checked `data_checksums` first. It was already `on`, the PostgreSQL 18 default.
- Ran a client on db2 that inserts a row every 0.2 s and logs the time, the id and the server ([`failover-client.sh`](../../scripts/failover-client.sh)).
- Killed the postmaster on db1, masked the unit, promoted db2 with `pg_promote()`. The client moved to db2 by itself.
- The last row db1 confirmed (id 631) was on db2. The next id was 648, a gap from the sequence, not lost rows.
- Rewound db1: the servers diverged at `0/340628B8` on timeline 3, and `pg_rewind` copied 181 MB of 340 MB.
- Switched back on purpose: stopped db2 cleanly, promoted db1, started db2 as its standby on timeline 5. No `pg_rewind` this time.
- Added `checks/16-failover.sh`. Checks 06, 14, 15 and 16 pass after a reboot of both hosts.

## What broke
- **On db2 the client got `no pg_hba.conf entry for host "192.168.122.12", user "remote_test", database "lab"`.** I had added the line but not reloaded. db1 answered first, so the two-host connection string hid it; after a failover the client would have failed on db2.
- **After `pg_rewind`, db1's `primary_conninfo` pointed at `host=192.168.122.11`, db1 itself.** `pg_rewind` copies the config files from the source, so db1 got db2's `postgresql.auto.conf`. I replaced the two `primary_` lines before the start. It also emptied `pg_replslot`, so the slot `db2` on db1 was gone.
- **db2 as primary logged `archive command failed with exit code 127` every 30 s, but `pg_stat_archiver` showed `failed_count` 0.** pgBackRest is only on db1. With exit 127 the archiver dies with FATAL and restarts instead of counting a failure, so only the log showed it.
- **After the reboot check 06 failed on db2: `postgresql is enabled`.** I had stopped db2 with `disable --now`, and the later `start` ran it only until the reboot. `enable --now` fixed it. A planned switch only needs `stop`.
